Scam losses: the state counts 10.4 billion baht in ten months, a survey 65.86 billion in twelve

The ministry's count fell 42%, from 6,577 million baht in January to May to 3,801 million in June to October. In the survey of 1,290 Thai adults, 10% lost money and 4% reported a scam to the authorities.

Scams2026-11-16

Two counts of the money Thailand loses to scams came out in the same week. On 10 November, opening the Global Anti-Scam Summit Asia in Bangkok, Digital Economy and Society Minister Chaichanok Chidchob said losses from online scams fell from 6,577 million baht in January to May 2026 to 3,801 million baht in June to October, down more than 42%, or nearly 2,800 million baht. Deputy government spokesperson Ploythalay Lakshmeesaengchan gave the same figures on 14 November. By the day that is 43.6 million baht falling to 24.8 million. Both said more than 1 million mule accounts, accounts opened or rented out to receive stolen money, have been frozen through the AOC 1441 centre, which links the police, the Department of Special Investigation, the Anti-Money Laundering Office, the Bank of Thailand and the SEC.

On 9 November the Global Anti-Scam Alliance (GASA), with Mastercard and Whoscall, published State of Scams in Thailand 2026, a survey of 1,290 Thai adults. Its estimate of losses over the past year is 65.86 billion baht, about US$1.85 billion, with an average loss of 11,951.20 baht per victim. Across all respondents, 10% lost money or shared personal information that led to a loss, and 4% reported a scam to the authorities. Among those who lost money, 60% had lost money to scams more than once. Spread over a year, 65.86 billion baht is about 180 million baht a day.

The two are not one series. The ministry's figure counts losses in cases that reached the authorities; the survey's is an estimate built from what people said they lost, reported or not. The ministry's ten months add up to 10,378 million baht, about 34 million baht a day, and the survey's day is about five times that. The size of a loss differs too: the Thailand Consumers Council put the average at above 52,000 baht a case for the cases recorded in January to June 2026, against the survey's 11,951 baht per victim.

On how the money left, the survey has bank transfers and other electronic transfers in 49% of cases, against an Asian average of 36%, and digital wallets second at 29%. Scammers reached people in Thailand most often through Facebook (54%) and LINE (48%). The next door to narrow is the one between a Thai exchange and a stranger's wallet: on 12 November the SEC opened comment, until 26 November, on a draft that lets stablecoins such as USDT move only between a customer's own accounts and wallets, with a cap of 5 million baht a day at each licensed firm. The SEC says the draft is aimed at money laundering, cybercrime and ways around the rules on sending money abroad.

Shape: Falling
Speed: months

Falling, in months, on the count the state publishes: 43.6 million baht a day in January to May, 24.8 million in June to October. The survey is one point, a year wide, so it has no shape yet; it has a size, about five times the reported count. The guess: the reported count goes on falling into 2027 and the survey total moves much less. It rests on three numbers. Reports are what the ministry counts, and 4% of the people surveyed made one. The losses that do get reported are the large ones, above 52,000 baht a case against 11,951 baht per victim in the survey, and large transfers are what a frozen mule account stops. And 49% of the money still left by bank transfer after more than a million accounts were frozen, with wallets at 29%; as bank accounts close, the guess is that the wallet share grows, which is where the SEC's own-wallet draft and the Travel Rule of 27 February 2027 land. The next numbers to set beside these: the ministry's November to March figure, and GASA's 2027 survey.

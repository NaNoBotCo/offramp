พระราชกำหนดการประกอบธุรกิจสินทรัพย์ดิจิทัล พ.ศ. 2561

Emergency Decree on Digital Asset Businesses

ตราขึ้นเป็นพระราชกำหนดตามคำแนะนำของคณะรัฐมนตรี อยู่ในความดูแลของกระทรวงการคลังและสำนักงานคณะกรรมการกำกับหลักทรัพย์และตลาดหลักทรัพย์ (ก.ล.ต.)

กำหนดนิยามของคริปโตเคอร์เรนซี โทเคนดิจิทัล และสินทรัพย์ดิจิทัล และกำหนดให้การประกอบธุรกิจศูนย์ซื้อขาย นายหน้า หรือผู้ค้าสินทรัพย์ดิจิทัล ต้องได้รับใบอนุญาตจากรัฐมนตรีว่าการกระทรวงการคลังตามข้อเสนอของ ก.ล.ต. (มาตรา 26) ผู้ประกอบธุรกิจดังกล่าวโดยไม่ได้รับอนุญาตมีโทษจำคุก 2 ถึง 5 ปี และปรับ 200,000 ถึง 500,000 บาท และปรับอีกไม่เกินวันละ 10,000 บาทตลอดเวลาที่ยังฝ่าฝืน (มาตรา 66) กรรมการที่รับผิดชอบในความผิดของบริษัทต้องรับโทษเช่นเดียวกัน (มาตรา 94) พระราชกำหนดนี้มีผลใช้บังคับตั้งแต่วันถัดจากวันประกาศในราชกิจจานุเบกษา[104][86]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: การซื้อ ถือ และขายคริปโตผ่านผู้ประกอบธุรกิจไทยที่ได้รับอนุญาต ทำได้โดยชอบด้วยกฎหมายสำหรับทุกคนที่ผู้ประกอบธุรกิจรายนั้นรับเป็นลูกค้า การรับซื้อหรือขายคริปโตให้ผู้อื่นเป็นธุรกิจประจำ รวมถึงการซื้อขายแบบบุคคลต่อบุคคลที่ไม่เป็นทางการ อยู่ในขอบเขตของใบอนุญาตผู้ค้าสินทรัพย์ดิจิทัล

2018-05-14มีผลบังคับ รายงานข่าว

Crypto gains as assessable income, 15% withholding

The Emergency Decree amending the Revenue Code (No. 19) B.E. 2561 adds two income types to Section 40(4): shares of profit or similar benefits from holding digital tokens (40(4)(g)), and gains from transferring cryptocurrency or digital tokens above cost (40(4)(h)). Both carry 15% withholding under Section 50(2)(f) for resident and non-resident individuals, creditable against the annual tax. Gains go into the annual personal income tax return at the progressive rates.[84][102]

ตัวอย่าง: Revenue Department rate table applied to 400,000 baht of net taxable income: 0% on the first 150,000; 5% on 150,001-300,000 (7,500 baht); 10% on 300,001-400,000 (10,000 baht); total 17,500 baht. Brackets run from exempt up to 150,000 baht to 35% above 4,000,000 baht. (Arithmetic applied to the RD table; the RD page gives no crypto example.)

2025-09-05มีผลบังคับ รายงานข่าว

Five-year exemption for crypto gains on licensed Thai platforms

Ministerial Regulation No. 399 (B.E. 2568), published in the Government Gazette on 5 September 2025, exempts individuals from personal income tax on gains from transferring cryptocurrency or digital tokens through an SEC-licensed digital asset exchange, broker or dealer, for transfers from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2029. Companies are excluded. The exemption turns on the sale going through a licensed Thai operator, so offshore, peer-to-peer and private sales outside licensed operators are not covered.[85][98]

ตัวอย่าง: Illustration, not from the sources: buy 1 BTC on a licensed Thai exchange for 2,000,000 baht and sell it there in 2026 for 2,600,000 baht; the 600,000 baht gain is exempt. The same sale on an offshore exchange gets no exemption under this regulation. After 31 December 2029 the exemption ends unless extended.

2024-01-01มีผลบังคับ

Foreign income taxed when brought in, in any year

Revenue Department Order Por. 161/2566, signed 15 September 2023, reads Section 41 paragraph 2 of the Revenue Code so that a Thai tax resident who earns income from work, business or property abroad must add it to taxable income in the year it is brought into Thailand, whatever year it was earned. It applies to income brought in from 1 January 2024. Before this, income brought in after the year it was earned escaped Thai tax. Partial remittances are taxed pro rata.[81][82][83]

ตัวอย่าง: From the RD guide: income earned from 1 January 2024 by a foreigner who stayed 180 days or more in Thailand in the year of earning is taxed when brought in, even in a later year; income earned in a year of fewer than 180 days is not. A resident who earned 1,000,000 baht abroad in 2025 and transfers 400,000 baht of it to Thailand in 2026 reports the 400,000 baht for 2026 (arithmetic on the RD rule).

2023-11-20มีผลบังคับ

Income earned before 2024 stays outside the new rule

Order Por. 162/2566, dated 20 November 2023, amends Por. 161/2566 so it does not apply to assessable income that arose before 1 January 2024. Such income can be brought into Thailand in a later year without Thai tax under the old reading.[81][82][83]

ตัวอย่าง: Savings earned abroad in 2022 and moved to Thailand in 2026 are not taxed on arrival (RD guide, case 1). Proof of when the money was earned may be requested.

2024-09-24มีผลบังคับ

Tax residence: 180 days in a calendar year

An individual present in Thailand for 180 days or more in a calendar year, counted in total, is a Thai tax resident for that year and taxable on Thai-source income plus foreign income brought in. Non-residents pay tax only on Thai-source income. The RD's 2024 guide for foreigners says 180 days or more; the RD English personal income tax page says more than 180 days.[82][102][103]

ตัวอย่าง: RD guide, case 2: foreign income of a foreigner who was not a Thai tax resident when it was earned is not taxed when later brought into Thailand.

2025-06-04ร่าง/รับฟังความเห็น รายงานข่าว

Proposed exemption for foreign income brought in within two years

The Revenue Department's director-general, Pinsai Suraswadi, said in June 2025 the department was drafting a ministerial regulation to exempt foreign-sourced income brought into Thailand in the year it is earned or the following year; income brought in from the third year would be taxed normally, and the change would not be retroactive. The stated aim was repatriation of an estimated 2 trillion baht held abroad. As of July 2026 the measure had not been published in the Royal Gazette, according to Thai law firms; one attributes the stall to the dissolution of Parliament before the 8 February 2026 election.[103][156][157][81]

ตัวอย่าง: As described by Bangkok Post (not law): income earned abroad in 2025 and brought in during 2025 or 2026 would be exempt; brought in during 2027, taxed. Under the rule in force today, the 2025 income is taxed in whichever year it arrives.

2024-01-01มีผลบังคับ ยังไม่ยืนยัน

Crypto sold abroad and the proceeds brought in

No Revenue Department guidance specific to crypto sold on foreign platforms turned up in this research. Under the general rules, a gain above cost on crypto is assessable income (Section 40(4)(h)); for a Thai tax resident, gains treated as foreign-sourced become taxable when brought into Thailand under Por. 161/2566, unless earned before 1 January 2024 (Por. 162/2566). The MR 399 exemption covers only transfers through SEC-licensed Thai operators. Whether a gain on an offshore exchange is foreign-sourced, and how the gain portion of a partial transfer is measured, is not settled in the sources read.[84][81][82][85]

ตัวอย่าง: No worked example in the sources. The RD guide says a partial remittance is taxed pro rata and that tax paid abroad can be credited under a double tax agreement, capped at the Thai tax on that income.

2024-09-24มีผลบังคับ

Credit for foreign tax under double tax agreements

A Thai tax resident who paid tax abroad on income later brought into Thailand can credit that tax against Thai tax where a double tax agreement allows it. The credit cannot exceed the Thai tax on that income. Supporting documents must be in English or Thai; the RD recommends a tax payment certificate from the foreign tax authority.[82]

ตัวอย่าง: No numbers in the source.

2026-09-14ประกาศแล้ว

OECD crypto reporting (CARF): first exchanges by 2028

Thailand is on the OECD list of jurisdictions committed to the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, in the group undertaking first exchanges by 2028 (27 jurisdictions, including Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong and the UAE). Under CARF, crypto service providers report customers' transactions to their tax authority, which exchanges them with the customer's country of residence. The Thai domestic law and the first reporting year were not found in this research.[10]

ตัวอย่าง: No numbers in the source. Under the framework, once exchanges start, a CARF-reporting platform abroad reports a Thai tax resident's crypto transactions to its own tax authority, which passes them to the Revenue Department; Thai platforms report non-residents' data the other way.

2024-08-01

Mule accounts handled at the person level, cross-bank

The BOT raised mule-account management from the account to the individual: from 1 August 2024 all commercial banks share suspect data through the Central Fraud Registry (CFR). When a person is flagged, banks suspend electronic channels on all that person's accounts until verification and can refuse new accounts. Where a bank sees suspicious behaviour without a complaint, it suspends electronic transactions at minimum. Holders of accounts named in police reports or by AMLO must contact the Anti-Online Scam Operation Center on 1441.[99]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: One flagged account can freeze app and online access at every Thai bank the person uses, and block opening a new one.

2025-01-30

Wider mule-account screening from January 2025

Banks must suspend outgoing transfers and refuse new accounts for people whose accounts carry a high risk of mule use even before any victim reports a loss; block money coming into clearly identified mule accounts; and warn senders who may be paying a mule account. Detection criteria now include transfer patterns and amounts, and banks share lists of people showing suspicious behaviour, not only those in AMLO or fraud reports.[90]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: Frequent incoming transfers from unrelated people, as in peer-to-peer crypto selling, fit the transfer patterns banks screen for, and an account can be restricted without a police complaint.

2023-03-09

Mobile banking limits and face scans

BOT minimum standards: banks may not send links by SMS or email; one mobile banking user per app and one device; notification of every transaction; a facial biometric check for opening an account through an app, for transfers above 50,000 baht per transaction, and for raising the daily transfer limit above 50,000 baht. Banks must detect and temporarily hold suspicious transactions in near real time and run a 24-hour fraud hotline.[80]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: Large app transfers and limit changes need a face scan matched to the ID on file, typically the passport for foreigners.

2023-03-17

Penalties for lending, renting or selling an account

Opening or letting someone use a bank, e-money or crypto account for crime: up to 3 years, up to 300,000 baht, or both. Arranging or advertising the purchase, sale, rent or loan of accounts: 2 to 5 years, 200,000 to 500,000 baht, or both. Crypto accounts were added by the April 2025 amendment.[91][92][93]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: The offences apply to anyone in Thailand, foreign nationals included.

2026-09-10

Financial-sector framework against illicit activity

The BOT and 11 industry bodies, including the Thai Bankers' Association, foreign bank and e-payment associations and non-bank lenders, adopted a framework to tighten KYC, CDD and enhanced due diligence, scrutinise high-risk cash transactions, build a database of high-risk individuals linked to the Central Fraud Registry, and issue industry anti-money-laundering guidelines.[158]

สำหรับชาวต่างชาติ: Account opening and reviews for customers treated as higher risk are set to get stricter across banks and e-money providers.

คำถามที่ยังเปิดอยู่

  • TouristDigiPay live status: the SEC's English news from September 2025 to 23 September 2026 names no approved operator, e-money partner or launch date. Bitkub and Binance TH announced interest in 2025. Whether any service is live in September 2026 is not confirmed.
  • Ministerial Regulation No. 399 was not read in the Royal Gazette; its date (5 September 2025), period and conditions come from Tilleke & Gibbins and HLB Thailand.
  • The Emergency Decree on Digital Asset Businesses and the Revenue Code amendment (No. 19) were not read in the Gazette; the 13 May 2018 publication and 14 May 2018 start dates come from Forvis Mazars (for the tax decree) and the SEC translation's 'day following publication' clause.
  • Whether the Revenue Department treats gains on crypto sold on a foreign exchange as foreign-sourced income under Section 41, and how the gain share of a partial transfer is measured, was not found in any RD publication.
  • Whether the 15% withholding on crypto gains applies to trades on licensed Thai exchanges, and whether a VAT exemption for crypto transfers through licensed exchanges is in force, were not verified.
  • Loss offsetting for crypto (reported in 2022 for trades on licensed exchanges) was not verified.
  • The remittance exemption for foreign income brought in within the year earned or the next: last confirmed status is 'not in the Royal Gazette as of July 2026' from law firms; no Revenue Department or Gazette text was found. The RD's Por. 161/2566 file, re-uploaded in June 2026, shows no change.
  • Thai domestic law for CARF, and the first year Thai platforms must collect data, were not found; the OECD list gives only 'first exchanges by 2028'.
  • G-Token: the SEC rules took effect 21 July 2025, but whether the Ministry of Finance has issued a G-Token, its size, and whether foreigners may buy it were not confirmed.
  • Bitkub IPO: no SEC or SET filing or major-outlet report was read in this research; status unknown.
  • Upbit Thailand, Bitkub, Orbix, WAAN and other licensed exchanges' rules for foreign customers were not checked; only Binance TH's published KYC rules were read. Bitkub's help site appears to carry a KYC guide for foreign nationals, but it was not read.
  • Whether any Thai exchange pays baht to a foreign (non-Thai) bank account was not found.
  • Bank-by-bank branch practice for retirement, LTR, Thailand Privilege and DTV holders beyond the published pages of Kasikornbank, SCB and Bangkok Bank is not verified; reports from forums and agents were not used.
  • Reports of Thai banks freezing accounts that receive peer-to-peer crypto payments were not traced to a primary or major-outlet source; the BOT rules that allow such freezes are sourced.
  • PromptPay registration rules for foreigners (for example, linking a Thai mobile number) were not verified.
  • Any limit on the number of bank accounts one person may open was not found in BOT releases read.
  • KBank's Q Wallet USDC-to-THBS pilot was scheduled to end on 31 December 2025; whether it was extended is not known.
  • The Section 26/1 start date differs by source: the SEC gives 13 April 2025; DFDL writes that the decree took effect on publication (12 April 2025). The SEC translation says the day after publication.
  • The investor-alert date for Bybit could not be found; Bybit does not appear on the SEC digital-asset alert list viewed on 23 September 2026 even though it is blocked and under criminal complaint.
  • The 2013 Bitcoin Co. Ltd episode is from memory of press coverage and was not re-verified.

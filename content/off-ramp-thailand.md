Cashing out crypto in Thailand splits into two very different situations, and almost
every bad guide blurs them:

- **You have a Thai bank account** (you're on a long-stay visa, married, work permit,
  etc.). You have the cheapest routes available — sell peer-to-peer and receive baht by
  PromptPay in minutes.
- **You do not have a Thai bank account** (tourist, new arrival, digital nomad on a
  short visa). The bank-linked routes are closed to you — but you are *not* stuck. Crypto
  debit cards, cash P2P, and Bitcoin ATMs all work without one.

:::key First, answer one question
**Do you have a Thai bank account?** It decides your whole route. PromptPay, the fast
cheap rail everyone raves about, is tied to a Thai bank account or Thai national ID — so
"just use PromptPay" is useless advice if you don't have either. This page keeps the two
paths separate so you don't chase a road that's blocked for you.
:::

## How to off-ramp crypto to baht (residents with a Thai bank)

This is the cheapest path when you have a Thai bank account.

1. **Hold a stablecoin, not a volatile coin, while you cash out.** Convert to USDT or
   USDC first so the price can't swing mid-trade. Learn the difference in
   [[get-paid-in-crypto|Get paid in crypto 101]].
2. **Fund a reputable exchange with a strong Thailand P2P book** — for example
   %%AFF:binance|Binance%% or %%AFF:bybit|Bybit%%. Double-check you're sending the coin on
   the **right network** (see [[scams|Stay safe]]).
3. **Open the P2P section and choose "Sell."** Filter for THB, PromptPay or your bank,
   and a merchant with a high completion rate and thousands of trades.
4. **Match with a buyer and release only after the baht lands.** The platform holds your
   crypto in escrow; confirm the THB is actually in your bank app *before* you release.
   Never release on a screenshot.
5. **Keep a simple record** — date, amount, rate — for your own taxes back home. Tools
   like %%AFF:koinly|Koinly%% can pull this together later.

:::tip Pick merchants like you pick a street-food stall
High volume, long history, lots of happy trades. A rate that looks too good usually
comes with a slow "payment sent… trust me" merchant. Boring and busy beats cheap and
sketchy.
:::

## If you don't have a Thai bank account

You have three solid no-bank routes. None of them need a Thai bank.

**1. Spend it directly with a crypto debit card.** A card funded from your crypto
balance lets you tap-to-pay at shops and withdraw baht from ATMs, converting on the fly.
This is the closest thing to "crypto in, baht out" for a nomad. Which cards actually
work in Thailand — and the fees to watch — is its own page: [[crypto-cards|Crypto cards
that work in Thailand]].

**2. Cash P2P — sell for physical baht.** On the same P2P platforms above, some
merchants offer **cash in person**: you meet, they hand you baht, you release the crypto
from escrow. Good for larger amounts. Meet in a safe public place (a mall, a bank lobby),
never release before you've counted the cash, and read the safety notes in
[[scams|Stay safe]] first.

**3. Bitcoin / crypto ATMs.** A handful exist in Bangkok, Chiang Mai and tourist areas.
Convenient and no-bank, but the spread is steep — fine for a small top-up, painful for
real money. *Inference: availability and machines change often; confirm one is operating
before you rely on it.*

:::money The realistic no-bank playbook
For everyday spending, a **crypto card** is the workhorse. For a bigger lump sum, line up
a trusted **cash-P2P merchant or an OTC desk**. Skip the ATM unless it's a small, "I need
500 baht right now" moment.
:::

## The fiat leg: getting value *home* vs spending it *here*

Two different goals people mix up:

- **Spending in Thailand** → the routes above (card, P2P, cash).
- **Moving value back to your home country** → cash out to USD/EUR on a global exchange
  like %%AFF:kraken|Kraken%%, then use a multi-currency account like %%AFF:wise|Wise%% to
  send it onward cheaply. This isn't "off-ramping to baht" — it's the other direction, and
  it's often the right move for savings rather than daily cash.

## A word on Thai law and tax

Thailand regulates crypto through the SEC and licenses local exchanges; rules on
foreigners, reporting, and remitted income have been changing. *Inference — this is a
moving target.* Nothing here is legal or tax advice. If you're a US person, remember your
US filing obligations follow you abroad (income, and forms like FBAR/FATCA can apply). Get
the specifics from a professional, and keep clean records from day one.

## FAQ

### Can I cash out crypto in Thailand without a Thai bank account?
Yes. A Thai bank account unlocks the cheapest route (P2P paid by PromptPay), but without
one you can still spend crypto through a crypto debit card, sell for physical cash via
in-person P2P, or use a Bitcoin ATM. The bank only gates the bank-based rails, not crypto
itself.

### Is PromptPay usable if I'm a tourist?
Generally no — PromptPay links to a Thai bank account or Thai national ID, which tourists
usually don't have. Plan around crypto cards and cash P2P instead. *Inference — confirm
current bank onboarding rules, which change.*

### What's the cheapest way to turn crypto into baht?
For residents, selling a stablecoin peer-to-peer and receiving PromptPay is typically
near the market rate with only a small spread. For non-residents, a crypto debit card is
usually the most practical, though card FX and ATM fees add up — compare them on the
[[crypto-cards|crypto cards]] page.

### How do I avoid losing money on the transfer itself?
Match the coin *and* the network *and* the address format on both ends before you send,
and start with a tiny test amount for any new address. Sending on the wrong network is the
single most common way people lose funds. See [[scams|Stay safe]].

### Should I hold Bitcoin or a stablecoin while cashing out?
A stablecoin (USDT/USDC) while you're actively off-ramping, so a price swing can't shrink
your baht between steps. Hold volatile assets on purpose, not by accident during a cash-out.

For anyone in Thailand **without a Thai bank account**, a crypto debit card is usually the
most practical way to spend. It looks like a normal Visa or Mastercard; behind it sits your
crypto (or a stablecoin balance), converted to baht at the moment you tap.

:::key What "works in Thailand" actually means
Three things have to line up: the card must (1) **accept your country** at signup and KYC,
(2) run on a **network Thai terminals and ATMs accept** (Visa/Mastercard — nearly all do),
and (3) let you **fund it with the coin you hold.** A card can be brilliant and still be
unavailable to your passport. Always confirm your country on the issuer's live page before
you commit — this space changes constantly.
:::

## How a crypto debit card works

1. You load it — either from a **custodial balance** on an exchange/app, or directly from
   your **own self-custody wallet** at the moment of payment.
2. You tap to pay, or withdraw baht at an ATM.
3. The card converts your crypto/stablecoin to baht at that instant, at the network FX rate
   plus the issuer's spread.

The magic is the on-the-fly conversion. The catch is that the conversion, the FX, and the
ATM all take a small cut — and those cuts are where cards quietly differ.

## The fees nobody prints on the box

- **FX / conversion spread** — the gap between the real rate and the rate you get. The
  single biggest cost on everyday spending. *Inference — often the deciding factor between
  two otherwise similar cards.*
- **ATM withdrawal fee** — a flat fee and/or a percent, sometimes with a small monthly
  free allowance. Thai ATMs *also* charge foreign-card fees (commonly ~220 THB per
  withdrawal). Big withdrawals beat many small ones.
- **Load / top-up fee** — some cards charge to move crypto onto the card.
- **Inactivity or monthly fee** — rarer now, but check.
- **Reward rate** — some pay cashback in a token; nice, but don't let a flashy reward
  distract from a fat FX spread you pay on every purchase.

:::money The comparison that matters
For daily spending, rank cards by **FX spread first, ATM fees second, rewards last.** A 2%
FX spread on everything you buy dwarfs a 1% cashback you only sometimes earn.
:::

## Custodial vs self-custody cards

- **Custodial** (the exchange holds your balance): easiest to set up, one account for
  trading *and* spending — for example the %%AFF:bybit_card|Bybit Card%% sits on top of a
  Bybit balance. Trade-off: your funds live on the platform.
- **Self-custody** (settles from your own wallet): the card pulls from a wallet you
  control, so you're not parking a balance with anyone — %%AFF:gnosispay|Gnosis Pay%% is the
  best-known example. Trade-off: a little more setup, and you manage your own keys (get a
  [[get-paid-in-crypto|hardware wallet]] if you go this way).

Neither is "better" — it's the custodial-convenience versus self-custody-control trade you
make everywhere in crypto.

## Before you order any card

1. **Confirm your country is supported** on the issuer's live signup page.
2. **Read the fee schedule** — FX spread, ATM fee, load fee — and screenshot it.
3. **Check funding coins** — does it take the stablecoin you hold, on a network you can send?
4. **Order, KYC, and load a small amount first.** Do one small test purchase and one small
   ATM pull before you rely on it for rent money.
5. **Keep the bulk of your crypto off the card.** A card is a spending float, not a vault —
   load what you'll spend, keep savings in your own [[get-paid-in-crypto|wallet]].

## FAQ

### Can I use a crypto debit card in Thailand without a Thai bank account?
Yes — that's the main reason to get one. The card runs on Visa/Mastercard rails, so it
works at Thai shops and ATMs like any foreign card, funded by your crypto instead of a
bank. No Thai bank account required.

### Which is cheaper day to day — a crypto card or P2P?
For residents with a Thai bank, selling P2P to PromptPay is usually cheaper per baht. For
non-residents, a crypto card is the practical winner because it needs no local bank — just
mind the FX spread and ATM fees. See the [[off-ramp-thailand|off-ramp Thailand]] matrix.

### What fees should I expect at a Thai ATM?
Expect the card issuer's own ATM fee *plus* the Thai bank's foreign-card fee (commonly
around 220 THB per withdrawal). Because that Thai fee is roughly fixed per withdrawal,
fewer, larger withdrawals cost less overall. *Inference — confirm the current amount at the
machine.*

### Custodial or self-custody card — which should I pick?
If you want the simplest setup and already trade on an exchange, a custodial card is
easiest. If you'd rather not leave a balance on a platform, a self-custody card that settles
from your own wallet fits better. Match it to how much control you want over your keys.

### Are the rewards worth it?
Treat cashback as a tie-breaker, not a reason to choose. A high FX conversion spread will
quietly cost you more on every purchase than a headline reward pays back.
